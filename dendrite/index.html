Dendrite — Diffusion-Limited Aggregation

A particle appears at a random point on a circle. It walks — one step north, south, east, or west, chosen by a coin flip. It walks until it bumps into the cluster. Then it sticks. A new particle appears. Repeat ten thousand times.

That's it. That's the entire algorithm. Two sentences produce a fractal.

The branches are not designed. They emerge from a single bias: a random walker is more likely to hit a protruding part of the cluster than a recessed part. The tips catch more particles than the crevices. Growth concentrates at the extremities. The branches reinforce themselves — a tip that sticks out gets bigger, which makes it stick out more, which catches more walkers. This is called screening, and it is why the structure is branched rather than compact.

The result has a fractal dimension of about 1.71 in 2D — between a line (1.0) and a filled disk (2.0). Not an integer. Not designed. Measured.

The same process grows soot in a flame, coral on a reef, lightning from a cloud, snowflakes from moisture, mineral dendrites in rock fractures, and the blood vessels in your eye. Nature uses this recipe everywhere it needs to fill space from a point, and it never writes a blueprint. The diffusion does the design.

The mechanism

Each particle is a random walk on a grid — at each step it moves one cell in a random cardinal direction. When it steps onto a cell adjacent to the cluster, it sticks with probability p (the stickiness). At p = 1, every contact sticks; lower values let particles penetrate deeper before adhering, producing denser clusters. Particles that wander beyond twice the spawn radius are killed and respawned — without this, walkers could drift forever in empty space, and the simulation would grind to a halt.

The spawn radius tracks the cluster's furthest reach, so new particles are always released just outside the existing structure. The fractal dimension is estimated by box-counting: overlay grids of different cell sizes, count how many contain cluster cells, and fit the scaling. A slope of 1.71 means the cluster fills space like a crumpled sheet, not like a solid or a line.

The question, same as every piece in this gallery: where does order come from when nobody's in charge? Here the answer is: from diffusion. The randomness IS the architect. The random walk delivers particles to the tips preferentially, and the tips grow. Nobody chose the branches. The random walk chose them.
